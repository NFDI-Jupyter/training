# For trainers and workshop organisers

The training modules are designed for self-study, but they can also support instructor-led workshops, demonstrations, and training events.

## Using the modules in a workshop

- **Select a learning outcome.** Choose one or two modules relevant to your audience rather than attempting to cover everything.
- **Check access in advance.** Make sure participants can log in and launch an environment before the hands-on exercises.
- **Build in time for practice.** Allow participants to use their own code or datasets when practical.
- **Point to the original materials.** The linked Jupyter Books can serve as references after the workshop.
- **Check the licence.** The modules are published in separate repositories and their licensing terms may differ. Consult each repository before adapting or redistributing content.

## A possible workshop structure

| Part | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome and setup | Sign in, launch an environment, introduce JupyterLab |
| Guided demonstration | Work through the key concepts in a selected module |
| Hands-on practice | Participants work through the exercises |
| Apply and discuss | Try the workflow with participants' own projects |
| Wrap-up | Share next steps and links to self-study resources |

See also the [module catalogue](modules.md) and [learning paths](learning-paths.md).
